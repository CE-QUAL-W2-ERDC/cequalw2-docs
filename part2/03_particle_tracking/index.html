    Particle Tracking Algorithm¶

    The original particle tracking algorithm in CE-QUAL-W2 was developed by Goodwin et al. (2001) for use in a smart-particle tracking algorithm that predicts the movement of fish in a southern reservoir in the United States. This chapter describes the mathematical formulation for tracking discrete particles through the CE-QUAL-W2 computational grid.

    Algorithm Description¶

    The particle-tracking algorithm was developed by Chapman et al. (1994) to predict transport and fate of floatables and suspended and dissolved materials in three dimensions. The algorithm was simplified for use in CE-QUAL-W2 by removing portions of the model relating to surface transport of floatables and suspended and dissolved materials. Transport equations for lateral movement were also removed because CE-QUAL-W2 computes forcing functions only in the longitudinal and vertical directions.

    Advective Transport Equations¶

    The following equations are used for particle tracking in CE-QUAL-W2 based on velocity:

    \[ X^{t+1} = X^{t} + U^{t}(Z^{t}, X^{t}) \cdot \Delta t \tag{3-1} \]
    \[ Z^{t+1} = Z^{t} + W^{t}(Z^{t}, X^{t}) \cdot \Delta t \tag{3-2} \]

    Variable Definitions¶

    The variables in the transport equations are defined as follows:

    Variable Definition
    Node(k,i) Node above, upstream, and closest to the particle's position, defined as the upper-left node of the rectangular cell formed by intersection of vertical layer k and longitudinal segment i
    \(x_{\text{Node}(k,i)}\) Longitudinal position of Node(k,i) (m)
    \(\Delta x\) Offset (downstream) from Node(k,i) to the particle's position (m)
    \(z_{\text{Node}(k,i)}\) Vertical position of Node(k,i) (m)
    \(\Delta z\) Offset (below) from Node(k,i) to the particle's position (m)
    \(X^{t}\) Longitudinal position of particle at time \(t\) (m); defined as \(x_{\text{Node}(k,i)} + \Delta x\) at time \(t\)
    \(Z^{t}\) Vertical position of particle at time \(t\) (m); defined as \(z_{\text{Node}(k,i)} + \Delta z\) at time \(t\)
    \(X^{t+1}\) Longitudinal position of particle at time \(t+1\) (m)
    \(Z^{t+1}\) Vertical position of particle at time \(t+1\) (m)
    \(U^{t}(Z^{t}, X^{t})\) Interpolated horizontal flow velocity (m/s) at location \((Z^{t}, X^{t})\)
    \(W^{t}(Z^{t}, X^{t})\) Interpolated vertical flow velocity (m/s) at location \((Z^{t}, X^{t})\)
    \(\Delta t\) Time interval between time \(t\) and time \(t+1\) (seconds)

    Boundary Checks¶

    After \(X^{t+1}\) and \(Z^{t+1}\) are calculated, boundary checks determine whether the particle's new position exceeds the longitudinal or vertical boundaries of the cell containing \(X^{t}\) and \(Z^{t}\), or any system boundary. If the new position exceeds the boundaries of the previous cell, an algorithm identifies the appropriate cell in which to place the particle. Once the correct cell is found, the values of k, i, Node(k,i), \(x_{\text{Node}(k,i)}\), \(z_{\text{Node}(k,i)}\), \(\Delta x\), and \(\Delta z\) are updated for the particle at \(t+1\) and stored.

    Since the displacement of a particle is proportional to the time step used, the displacement calculated in Equations 3-1 and 3-2 could position the particle outside system boundaries. To correct for this, the model places a particle near the boundary at a user-specified distance if \(X^{t+1}\) or \(Z^{t+1}\) exceeds the system boundaries.

    Turbulent Diffusion¶

    In addition to advective transport, a random movement is added to represent the effects of turbulence. The magnitude of the random movement is proportional to the intensity of turbulence in the system. Following Dunsbergen and Stalling (1993) and Chapman et al. (1994), the transport in x and z including both advection and random turbulent transport is:

    \[ X^{t+1} = X^{t} + U \Delta t + R\sqrt{6 D_x \Delta t} \tag{3-3} \]
    \[ Z^{t+1} = Z^{t} + W \Delta t + R\sqrt{6 D_z \Delta t} \tag{3-4} \]

    where:

    • \(R\) = a random number having a uniform distribution with zero mean and unit variance
    • \(D_x\) = longitudinal mass/heat dispersion coefficient
    • \(D_z\) = vertical mass/heat dispersion coefficient

    The use of the uniform distribution rather than a Gaussian distribution saves computational time and does not affect the resulting random distribution (Hathhorn, 1997).

    Dispersion Coefficient Specification¶

    The value of \(D_x\) is specified in the control file w2_con.npt, whereas \(D_z\) is computed by the turbulence closure model selected for the simulation (for example, setting [AZC] to TKE activates the k-ε turbulence model). A high value of \(D_x\) can produce excessive random longitudinal movement. In all cases, the maximum displacement of a particle per time step is constrained by the grid cell height (\(\Delta z\)) and segment length (\(\Delta x\)).

    High Dispersion Coefficient Values

    Setting \(D_x\) to a high value can cause excessive random longitudinal movement of particles. The maximum particle displacement per time step is bounded by the grid cell dimensions (\(\Delta z\) and \(\Delta x\)).

    Lateral Transport¶

    Particles also move in the lateral direction based on the following equation, assuming that the lateral dispersion coefficient is the same as the longitudinal coefficient:

    \[ Y^{t+1} = Y^{t} + V \Delta t + R\sqrt{6 D_x \Delta t} \tag{3-5} \]

    The value of \(V\) is based on any lateral inflows or outflows at a segment.

    Particle Location in the Grid¶

    The location of each particle in the grid is identified by segment (I) and layer (K) indices as well as X, Y, and Z coordinates within each cell. The standard CE-QUAL-W2 sign convention is followed in this algorithm:

    • Longitudinal velocity: positive downstream (increasing segment number)
    • Vertical velocity: positive downward

    Initial x, y, and z coordinates are specified for each particle released into a segment and layer. The user specifies x and z; y is set to the middle of the layer, that is, \(y = B/2\) where \(B\) is the layer width.

    Plan View Coordinates¶

    Within a given layer K and segment I, the coordinate system for the plan view is defined as follows:

    • X-coordinate: Ranges from 0 at the right (looking downstream) to DLX at the left, with DLX\(/2\) at the center.
    • Y-coordinate: Ranges from 0 at the left bank (looking upstream) to \(B\) at the right bank, with \(B/2\) at the center.

    Side View Coordinates¶

    Within a given layer K and segment I, the coordinate system for the side view is defined as follows:

    • Z-coordinate: Ranges from 0 at the top of the layer to DZ at the bottom, with DZ\(/2\) at the midpoint.
    • Y-coordinate: Same convention as the plan view (0 to \(B\)).

    Lateral and Longitudinal Velocities¶

    The velocity sign conventions within each cell are:

    • Longitudinal velocity: Positive in the downstream direction (toward segment I+1), negative in the upstream direction.
    • Lateral velocity: Positive to the right (looking downstream), negative to the left.
    • Withdrawals: Always assumed to be on the left-hand side (LHS) of the segment.
    • Side branches: Can connect on either the left-hand side (LHS) or right-hand side (RHS) of a segment.
